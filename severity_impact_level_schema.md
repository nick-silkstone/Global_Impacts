# Impact Level Schema

Impact levels are quite subjective, but the broad criteria below are used as
guidance when assessing whether events constitute very low, low, medium, or
high impacts. See `severity_classification_addendum.md` for how to apply
this under incomplete reporting, country-relative calibration, and
multi-country events.

## Very Low Impacts

| Impact Type | Typical impacts |
|---|---|
| Casualties | 0 (low risk 1–2) |
| Displaced | None, or very few for a short time. |
| Support required | Local support, e.g. local police and fire and rescue services. |
| Recovery time | Days |
| Costs | Minimal, even at the scale of regional authorities |

Example: localised heavy rainfall flooding a small number of properties or
roads for a short duration. **Not covered as standard** — excluded from
`latest_impacts.csv` logging by design.

## Low Impacts

| Impact Type | Typical impacts |
|---|---|
| Casualties | <10 (often just 1–2) |
| Displaced | Pre-emptive evacuations of a small number of people; short-term displacement of a few people (damaged housing). |
| Support required | Local support, e.g. local police and fire and rescue services. |
| Recovery time | Days / weeks |
| Costs | Minimal at the scale of a nation. |

Example: localised heavy rainfall flooding part of a town, with some
casualties (e.g. a car driving into flood water, or a landslide impacting a
road). Flooding subsides quickly; infrastructure back in action within days
or weeks.

## Medium Impacts

| Impact Type | Typical impacts |
|---|---|
| Casualties | Typically 10–99 |
| Displaced | Pre-emptive evacuations, many people at greatest exposure to the impacts and/or a proportion of the population remains displaced for weeks, with strained sheltering requirements. |
| Support required | National level support, e.g. deployment of a country's military to an area; central government funding for recovery. |
| Recovery time | Months |
| Costs | Typically <<1% GDP |

Example: very heavy rainfall flooding many properties across multiple towns
and large agricultural areas. Multiple casualties from cars being washed
away (perhaps bridges failing), landslides, or drowning. Flooding subsides
in days or a week; infrastructure takes weeks or months to repair. Short-term
clean water supply impacted; loss of crops/livestock requires some
additional recovery support.

## High Impacts

| Impact Type | Typical impacts |
|---|---|
| Casualties | Typically >100 |
| Displaced | Pre-emptive evacuations of a large proportion of the regional population and/or many people displaced for months, with sheltering requirements significantly exceeding shelter capacity (tented villages likely required). |
| Support required | International support/aid/finance for months, perhaps years. |
| Recovery time | Years |
| Costs | Typically >1% GDP |

Example: exceptionally heavy rainfall and flooding across a wide area for
many weeks — whole towns/cities and much agricultural area under deep
water. Large numbers of casualties from deep/fast-flowing water, multiple
bridge failures, some properties and roads washed away. Infrastructure
damage so significant it takes months or years to repair/replace. Medium-term
loss of clean water supply; large loss of crops/livestock/farmland means
shelter and aid are required for a long duration. Significant cost to the
nation — lost GDP plus recovery costs affecting one or multiple regions'
prosperity for more than a year.

## Where are higher impact events more likely?

High impact assessments are more likely for smaller, more vulnerable (high
population density / poorer infrastructure) and less developed nations. A
compact hurricane, for example, is likely to cause High impacts for a small,
vulnerable, poorly developed Caribbean island nation — where international
support is much more likely required, rebuilding takes years, and the event
(if it hits tourism and agriculture) significantly costs the nation's small
GDP.

High impacts are rarely reached for larger, more resilient/stable/wealthy
countries (e.g. USA and much of Western Europe), where only the most severe
events, impacting large and more vulnerable population centres, are able to
achieve high impacts.
